Reflection<br>
When I started this course, I mostly thought of machine learning as picking the right algorithm. Working through the seven projects changed that view. The Machine Learning Review project took me through a complete workflow, from exploring the data and building a preprocessing pipeline to evaluating a model with cross-validation, and that same workflow came back in every module after it. In Classification, I learned that accuracy on its own can be misleading and that precision, recall, and the confusion matrix give a more honest picture of how a model performs. The Support Vector Machines and Decision Trees projects showed me how much a model depends on its hyperparameters and how easily it can overfit when it is given too much freedom. Dimensionality Reduction helped me understand how PCA can keep most of the useful information in a dataset while making it smaller and easier to visualize. The last two modules, Artificial Neural Networks and Natural Language Processing, were the most challenging for me, but they also connected the earlier ideas to the deep learning tools that are used in industry today.<br>

Along the way, I built skills that I can use outside the classroom. I became more comfortable with data preprocessing, such as handling missing values, scaling features, and encoding categorical data, and with building models in Scikit-Learn and Keras. I practiced tuning models by adjusting settings like tree depth, regularization strength, kernel parameters, and the number of layers and neurons, and I learned to judge the results with cross-validation, classification reports, and training history instead of relying on a single score. Visualization also became an important part of my work, because plots of the data, decision boundaries, explained variance, and model training often helped me understand a model better than the numbers alone. Writing the explanations in each notebook pushed me to think about what my results actually meant, not just to produce them.<br>

These projects prepare me for real-world machine learning and AI work because they follow the same steps a business problem would require: understanding the data, preparing it carefully, trying more than one model, and choosing a model that performs well and can be explained to others. In my professional work with business data and reporting, this course has given me a clearer picture of how predictive models can be built on top of that data and evaluated responsibly. I now feel more confident that I can take a dataset, build a reasonable model, test it properly, and explain the trade-offs to people who are not technical.<br>
